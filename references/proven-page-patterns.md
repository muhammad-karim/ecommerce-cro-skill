# Proven page patterns: the ATIDFA framework and recurring fixes

This file is the backbone of the audit. The approach is to **start from a proven page structure rather than guesswork**, because a page that follows a tested framework is far more likely to convert from day one. It's drawn from hundreds of published e-commerce redesigns and A/B tests. Everything is paraphrased; sources are at the bottom.

## 1. The ATIDFA framework

ATIDFA is an e-commerce adaptation of the classic AIDA model. Run it top to bottom down a landing page or PDP:

| Step | What it means | What to check |
|---|---|---|
| **A — Attention** | Above the fold. A cold visitor decides within about 3–5 seconds whether to stay | Value proposition, social proof, trust signals, emotive imagery, CTA all visible without scrolling |
| **T — Transformation** | A simple three-step path: how you get it, how it arrives, the end result. The last step describes the *benefit*, not just the deliverable | Is there a "how it works" in three steps that ends on the outcome? |
| **I — Interest** | Benefits first, then features. Visitors care about outcomes | Benefit bullets before spec tables |
| **D — Desire** | Testimonials, reviews, case studies, UGC | Real, specific, prominent proof |
| **F — FAQ** | Answer the objections that stop someone buying. Source them from real customer questions (shipping, refunds, sizing, timing) | Does the FAQ address real objections, or generic filler? |
| **A — Action** | One clear CTA, with directional cues (arrow or pointing emoji) and urgency words like "now" | Single, contrasting, repeated CTA |



## 2. Above-the-fold essentials

On product and landing pages, these should all be visible without scrolling on mobile:

- **Value proposition / benefit-driven headline.** For products, add the outcome to the title. A good formula for sales pages is timeframe + result + benefit.
- **Specific social proof.** A star rating *plus a number*, such as "Rated 4.8 by 12,000+ customers". Stars alone are weaker.
- **Trust signals.** Icons with short text for free shipping, returns, guarantee and secure checkout. Move these from the footer (where nobody looks) to directly under the navigation, as a **trust policy bar**.
- **Product or emotive imagery.** 1:1 product images with clear thumbnails or carousel arrows. Use lifestyle, UGC or outcome imagery, not just packshots. Don't let a huge hero image push the CTA below the fold.
- **Price, displayed clearly.** Show the exact saving as money ("Save £12"), not just a strikethrough. Do the maths for the shopper.
- **3–5 benefit bullets**, made scannable with icons or emojis, instead of paragraphs or hidden dropdowns.
- **A primary CTA** in a contrasting colour, full width on mobile, and ideally **sticky at the bottom of the screen on mobile**. Anchor it with payment logos and trust icons directly beneath.
- **Subtle urgency or scarcity** near the button, only when true: "In stock, ships today", "Low stock", "Viewed 1,200 times in the last 24h". Loud flashing banners no longer work.

## 3. Direct-response rules (landing pages)

- **One page, one goal, one CTA.** Remove anything competing: extra nav links, social share icons and widgets, phone numbers on paid-traffic pages, secondary forms.
- **Problem → authority → solution.** Name the pain, establish credibility, then present the offer. The layout stays the same across industries.
- **Keep it simple (KISS).** One product and one offer per page. Upsell *after* the first purchase rather than crowding the page with bundles and price points.
- **Video**: load it in a modal from a third-party host, and use at most 1–2 per page. A secondary "Watch video" button can be colour-flipped from the primary.
- **Emojis in CTA text** (👉 Add to cart, ⏰ Rush my order) — reported to lift click-through.
- **Testimonials above the fold** on lead-gen, presale and sales pages. Avoid stock photos. A review that mentions a minor drawback can read as more credible.
- **Test every browser and device.** Forms and buttons can silently break on one browser.
- **Page speed**: run PageSpeed Insights and act on it. A commonly cited claim is that each second saved lifts conversion 3–10%.

## 4. Recurring fixes from 100 published redesigns

These patterns recur across teardowns of (MVMT, Gymshark, ASOS, Harry's, Ridge, Chewy, Sol Apparel, Citizen Home and many more). Use them as a checklist of "usual suspects":

**Announcement bar / header**
- Make the announcement bar contrast with the site (a white bar on a white site disappears). Add an emoji, and make sure it shows on mobile.
- Add a secondary trust bar under the nav: free shipping, returns, guarantee.
- Repeat the free-shipping message inside the mega menu.

**Mega menu**
- Use thumb-sized spacing and add images to categories.
- Add a line of social proof.
- Separate revenue links (products, collections) from generic links (about, blog).
- Remove social icons and off-site links from the menu.

**Collection pages**
- Put the free-shipping notice and social proof at the top.
- Provide simple sort and filter options.
- Size product cards so **2×2 products show above the fold** on mobile.
- Add star ratings under each product card, and badges like "New arrival", "Most popular" and (if true) "Limited stock".

**Product pages**
- Use 1:1 main images with visible thumbnails or arrows.
- Move reviews up beside the title. Change star colour and add a number, such as "10,000+ reviews".
- Shorten long product titles without losing key information.
- Write a short, clear description up top. Don't hide key selling points in dropdowns.
- Use benefit bullets with icons.
- Show an "In stock" message with an icon.
- Show the exact saving.
- Add a "Free shipping" seal on the product image (instead of zero-benefit seals like "new look").
- Offer bundling and quantity options to raise AOV.
- Use **one primary CTA**. Remove a competing "Add to wishlist" button if it fights the main CTA.
- Show press and publication logos ("As featured in") **only if genuine**.

**Cart / cart drawer**
- Show a clear item count.
- Use a **dynamic free-shipping progress bar** ("You're £8 away from free delivery 🚚").
- Show star ratings on items in the cart.
- Add a cross-sell or order bump ("You may also like"). It pairs with the shipping bar: people add an item to qualify.
- Label the button "Continue to checkout" rather than a blunt "Checkout", and tell people what the next step is.
- Put trust and policy seals and payment logos under the button.

**Lead-gen pages / forms**
- Put a trust policy bar under the nav. Social proof matters as much for lead-gen as for e-commerce.
- Make forms visual: a clear title (the value proposition), visible steps, a clear CTA and trust messaging.
- Anchor the form with benefit bullets, plus numbers (customers helped, years in business).

## 5. Reviews and social proof

The **7-part anatomy of a high-converting review**:
1. Put a headline above the review that calls out its most persuasive point.
2. Wrap it in quotation marks so it reads as a testimonial.
3. Bold the key phrase, usually the result.
4. Show the first *and* last name (where the customer consents).
5. Place a trusted platform logo beside it (Trustpilot, Google).
6. For video testimonials, add the key quote as text underneath, since many people watch without sound.
7. Choose reviews that **answer real fears and doubts**, found by asking customers what nearly stopped them buying.

**Placement**: show a review summary above the fold, photo reviews below the images, trust badges near add-to-cart, a UGC gallery mid-page, and testimonials throughout.

## 6. Recent experiments (Aug–Oct 2026)

These are recent, specific tests. The revenue figures are self-reported single cases, so treat them as hypotheses worth testing, not guarantees.

| Test | What changed | Reported result |
|---|---|---|
| **Objection-busting review under add-to-cart** | One compact review card directly under the ATC button. Not the "best" review, but the one that answers the #1 hesitation, found via a post-purchase survey ("what nearly stopped you?"). Short quote, stars, first name, "verified" | Not quantified |
| **Objection review carousel** | A carousel of reviews filtered for the most common pre-purchase objection, placed where doubt arises | ~$63.7k/month |
| **Hero that answers 3 questions** | The hero states what it is, who it's for, and how big the decision is. The headline names the product, use case and fit detail. The CTA becomes a next step, e.g. "Find the size for your room" instead of "Buy now" | ~$78k/month (the button credited most) |
| **Relevant free bonus on the PDP** | A seasonally named PDF guide the buyer actually wants (a BBQ guide for a $429 smoker), shown as a visible banner with its cover above the price. "A discount lowers what they pay; a gift raises what they get" | ~$68k/month |
| **Comparison table** | A PDP table of *checkable* facts (safety rating, thickness, wash durability, warranty) vs a generic "typical product". Use only if you lead on measurable attributes | ~$32.9k/month |
| **Mega menu: trending row** | A row of 3 real products with prices at the top of the menu, plus a one-line description under each category | ~$106.9k/month (vintage clothing) |
| **Mega menu: bestsellers carousel** | Visual bestseller carousel at the top of the mobile menu, collections ordered by RPV, reassurance at the bottom | Reported |
| **Mega menu: remove off-site links** | Removed social icons from the menu and added category images | +$14.6k/month, +$0.23 RPV |
| **Unit prices on multipacks** | Show price per unit under each pack size (a variant metafield plus one theme line on Shopify) | Not quantified |
| **Collection sort by RPV** | Replace the default sort (Newest or Best selling) with a manual sort that puts the top revenue-per-session products in the first 4 slots. Re-rank monthly. Keep "New in" as a separate collection | Reported |
| **Cart reassurance (high-ticket)** | Selling points in the cart, a pageable upsell row, an **estimated delivery date as a specific day**, payment logos under the button | Forecast ~$1.5M/yr (stove brand) |
| **Full cost in cart** | Show items + delivery + wrap + duties = total, plus "nothing else added at checkout". If shipping is unknown, show "Delivery from £X". The *jump* in price at checkout, not its size, drives abandonment | Not quantified |
| **Announcement bar link** | Link only an underlined "Shop now" phrase, not the whole bar | ~$57k/month |
| **Gift wrap upsell in cart** | An optional add-on that customers asked for, placed in the cart where it's seen | +$58.9k/month, +$6.01 RPV |
| **Native vs builder landing pages** | Rebuilt a winning landing page natively in Shopify (Liquid) vs the same page in a page builder | Native: +9.9% CR, +20.9% RPV, +10% AOV |
| **Persona post-click journeys** | One product, several personas. Each ad leads to a matching landing experience (PDP vs listicle vs advertorial vs quiz) and the whole journey is tested | Not quantified |

Ideas repeatedly proposed as **first tests for a store with no data**:
- **Cart**: free-gift or shipping progress bar, cross-sells with ratings and one-click add, obvious savings, reassurance near the checkout button.
- **PDP**: trust signals above the title, more visible reviews, prominent savings, features rewritten as benefits, sensible variant defaults, and "subscribe" vs "one-time" shown as two distinct choices with "pause or cancel anytime".
- **Homepage**: immediate trust, lifestyle imagery, a benefit-led headline, one clear CTA, key selling points just below the fold.
